### 0.3 What the devnet MVP proves and does not prove

**Proves:** a position is physically invisible on public L1 and to non-members through ER RPC (real TDX on `devnet-tee-as.magicblock.app`); one tap via session keys; MWA/Seed Vault on Seeker; a liquidation crank in the TEE driven by the Pricing Oracle; after-the-fact disclosure via Magic Actions; a public pool aggregate.

**Does not prove:** real liquidity and fees (test mint, our own pool); anonymity set (testers are a handful); attestation of the validator code (MRTD/RTMR allowlist — v1); behaviour under correlated load; ER SLA.

### 0.4 Privacy wording (identical everywhere)

Privacy from trackers, copy-bots, liquidation hunters and from us. **Not** from Intel and not from the MagicBlock validator operator. A hardware guarantee, not a cryptographic one. `verifyTeeRpcIntegrity` checks the authenticity of the TDX quote but does not compare MRTD/RTMR against a code allowlist — that check is separate v1 work.

> "Hyperliquid hides nothing. NEAR × Hyperliquid hides *who*. Dexxer hides *what*."

## 1. MVP scope

### 1.1 In

- **Market:** one, SOL-PERP. BTC/ETH — `Market` accounts in the config, disabled.
- **Collateral:** our own devnet mint `dUSDC` (6 decimals), faucet in the app. Custody via Ephemeral SPL Token: tokens in the Global Vault on L1, in the ER — only the pool eATA.
- **Margin:** isolated; one position per `(user, market)`, long **or** short; flip = close + open. Leverage 1–10x. **Decision 04.10.2026: Isolated only, no Isolated/Cross switch.** Margin is pinned to `PositionSlot.margin`, liquidation is computed per slot (`liquidatable_now`), the `liquidation_check` task is per (trader, market) pair. Cross (one collateral per account, liquidation by the equity of the whole account) is needed by order-book exchanges with a shared collateral pool (Drift — cross by default with subaccounts for isolation; Zeta — `CrossMarginAccount`); perps with a counterparty pool and an oracle price, like ours, work on Isolated without a switch (Jupiter Perps — collateral and liquidation price per position, up to 6 positions; Flash Trade — isolated collateral). The switch from the CEX-type reference (backlog 6.B) is not implemented; the ticket gets a static "Isolated" label. Revisit only after mainnet, with a separate design (account equity formula from several feeds, a scheduler task per trader, a slot-closing rule).
- **Actions:** `open`, `increase`, `decrease`, `close`, `add_margin`. Market orders at mark only; the slippage parameter is checked by the program.
- **Price:** Pricing Oracle (Pyth Lazer SOL/USD) in the TEE. Mark = EMA(index), computed by the crank. Checks on every read: feed id, `posted_slot > 0`, staleness ≤ 2 s, confidence, index/mark deviation.
- **Pool:** one `Pool`, counterparty to all trades. OI cap, max position, utilization cap. Commit to L1 at a fixed 5 min interval: total collateral + coverage bucket, without a per-side breakdown.
- **Fees:** open/close 6 bps to the pool. Liquidation fee 100 bps.
- **Liquidation:** crank in the ER (High-precision Scheduling), trigger `equity < MMR × notional` at mark with a 2-tick hysteresis, full. In the same tx stream as scheduled actions.
- **Privacy:** all of our program's accounts in the ER are permissioned. `Position` — `is_private`, members `[owner, session_key, crank]` (the crank submits liquidation txs, hence a member), zero commits until close. ~~`UserAccount` commits only `free_margin`, `locked_margin`.~~ **(week 2, 21.09.2026 — revised)** see the new rule below: a private account is **not committed to L1 as-is at all**.
- **Privacy — fundamental rule (week 2, 21.09.2026):** in PER, privacy is **read filtering in the TEE/QFS, not encryption** (until MagicBlock `onchain-privacy`/`local-development`: QFS blocks `getAccountInfo`/gPA for non-members; the bytes are not encrypted). L1 has no filter — therefore **a raw private (permissioned) account is never committed to L1**: that would make its bytes public and destroy privacy, and the TEE does not let such a commit through (observed, risk #13). Design consequence: everything that must reach L1 (public aggregate, trustless-exit proof, 13F disclosure) goes out **only through a separate PUBLIC derived account** — `Pool` (aggregate), a future commitment/root account (exit proofs + `write_commitment` hashes). The private `Position`/`UserAccount`/`MarketRisk`/`DisclosureQueue` live in the TEE and never reach L1 in plain form. Trustless exit is built on a public balances root + the owner's merkle proof, **not** on commits of the raw `UserAccount`.
- **13F:** on close — Magic Action `write_commitment(nonce, keccak256(record ‖ salt))`; after `reveal_after_slot` the crank writes `Disclosure`. The delay is a parameter (demo: minutes; product: 30 days). **(week 3, 21.09.2026)** both Magic Actions ride on the commit of the public `Pool` (#13); `mark_committed` returns `Position → Empty` — removes the "one position per run" limit. Plus a public debt receipt `BalancesRoot` (hashed leaves, no L1 claim — the safe is in eSPL). Full design — §2.4.
- **Wallet:** MWA — one signature for onboarding (fallback two); then a session key. The fee payer is ours.
- **Mobile:** Expo/Android. Connect → Onboard/Deposit → Trade → Position → History → Settings. Local push without a server.
- **No backend.** Private data — from the TEE directly; public data — from devnet RPC. Crank — in the ER, fallback script outside.
- **Trustless exit:** `close` + `withdraw` through the ER without us. If the ER dies — margin from the last `UserAccount` commit; an open position is lost (stated plainly in the README).

### 1.2 Deliberately out (v1+)

Hedge mode / multiple positions per market · ~~limit/TP/SL~~ (done 04.10.2026, PR #14 — conditional orders) · funding, borrow · **Cross margin / Isolated/Cross switch (decision 04.10.2026, see §1.1 "Margin")** · partial liquidation · netting · external venue adapter · compliance gate · backend, indexer, WS server · MRTD allowlist · iOS · token, referrals · Surfpool.

**Two rules that make privacy structural:**

1. No account with position fields has a commit-policy before close. `Market`/`Pool` contain no per-user data. `MarketRisk` and `DisclosureQueue` are never committed at all.
2. Margin is **accounting, not tokens.** `open`/`close`/`add_margin` touch no token account, only private PDAs. Tokens move only on deposit and withdrawal, which are public on L1 anyway. Reason: the eATA belongs to the eSPL program → an `EphemeralPermission` cannot be created on it, and a user → pool transfer on every open would reveal the margin and the time.

   *Check 2 (19.09.2026):* eSPL balances in the ER turned out to be owner-scoped already at the RPC level — someone else's `getAccount` with their own valid TEE token returns `null` (`TokenAccountNotFoundError`), i.e. another owner's token account is invisible even as a balance. This is stronger than our minimum requirement, but **the rule stays unchanged**: we do not build margin privacy on this eSPL behaviour — it is not ours, not documented as a guarantee and may change; a transfer on every open would still reveal the action time through tx metadata (§2.3).

### 2.3 Leak model

| Channel | Visible | Hidden |
|---|---|---|
| L1 (Solscan) | deposit (wallet, amount, time), withdrawal, fact of delegation (PDA owner = `DELeGGvXpWV2fqJUhqcF5ZSYMS4JTLjteaAMARRSaeSh`), 5-min `Pool` aggregate, `locked_margin` in `UserAccount` (5 min), commitment hashes, `Disclosure` after reveal | everything about the position: market, side, size, entry, PnL, liq price, funding, the fact that a position exists |
| ER RPC non-member | **transaction metadata (check 6, 19.09.2026):** `getSignaturesForAddress(PROGRAM_ID)` returns to anyone (with or without a token) the full list of the program's signatures — `signature`, `slot`, `blockTime`, `err`, `confirmationStatus`; `getTransaction(sig)` for a known signature returns `slot`, `blockTime`, `meta.err`/`status`, `meta.fee`, `computeUnitsConsumed`. That is, the fact, time and success of an action are visible | account contents (`getAccountInfo` on a permissioned PDA → `null`) and tx contents: `accountKeys`, `instructions`, `logMessages`, `preBalances`/`postBalances`, token balances — all empty; `recentBlockhash` zeroed |
| eSPL transfers in ER | only deposit/withdrawal | margin on open/close |
| Global Vault | the total balance of everyone | — |
| Indirect | `withdrawal − deposit` = PnL over the period; `locked_margin × max_lev` = upper bound of size | — |

The residual `locked_margin` leak can be removed by committing `UserAccount` only on withdrawal/exit (the price — losing locked margin if the ER dies). v1: a user choice in settings.

**Tx metadata leak (check 6, 19.09.2026).** `getSignaturesForAddress` on a **PDA** returns an empty array `[]` — but to the owner as well, i.e. the ER simply does not index by PDA address; as a privacy signal this method proves nothing. The reliable owner/non-owner signal is `getAccountInfo` (check 1). The real channel is the **program id**: it is public, and its signature list is open to everyone, along with timing. We cannot hide the fact of activity itself; we only hide its content. See risk #4 in §7.1.

### 2.4 Week 3 design: 13F pipeline, `BalancesRoot`, exit **(week 3, 21.09.2026)**

> **Outdated since 30.09.2026 in the 13F part (§2.4.1) — see §2.9:** trades are no longer disclosed; `BalancesRoot` and exit remain, with the amendments of §2.9.2.

Approved by a brainstorm on 21.09 after finding #13 (§2.1 "fundamental rule": a raw private account never goes to L1). All week-3 L1 writes ride **only on the commit of the public `Pool`** as Magic Actions; hashes leave the TEE only as *arguments* of public L1 instructions.

**2.4.1 13F pipeline (core)**

| Step | Who / where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `close_position` / liquidation (exists) | session / crank, ER | writes `Position.closed = ClosedRecord{…, salt, nonce, reveal_after_slot}`, state `Closed`. New field `Position.commitment_written: bool = false` |
| `commit_aggregate` (extended) | fee-payer/crank, ER | the crank passes `remaining_accounts` = `Position`s with `Closed && !commitment_written` **and** `DisclosureQueue`s with due records (it is a member of both, finds them via gPA). The program in the TEE computes `hash = commitment_hash(record, salt)` (keccak256), adds `add_post_commit_actions(write_commitment(nonce, hash))` for positions and `write_disclosure(args, salt)` for due records (via `due_reveals`) to the commit of `Pool`+`BalancesRoot`, sets `commitment_written = true`. **Both kinds of actions are in one `commit_aggregate`, without a separate `reveal` instruction** (§2.4.1 below updated with the fact of implementation, Task 3). The total number of actions per call is `MAX_ACTIONS_PER_COMMIT = 4` (the measured real bridge cap is 28/29, §2.4.4 M-C; 4 kept with a large margin, because `write_commitment`/`write_disclosure` are more expensive in accounts than the measured action) |
| `write_commitment` | L1, `#[action]` | creates `Commitment [b"commit", hash]` **(seed — Task 8b, ruling 9; the hash, not the nonce)**. **Mandatory** `source_program` (`address = crate::ID`) before the escrow accounts (check 7) |
| `mark_committed` | crank, ER | the crank **observes** `Commitment` on base (ER does not read L1 — risk #2) and calls it; the program moves the `ClosedRecord` into the `DisclosureQueue` ring, `Position → Empty`. **Removes the "one position per trader per run" limitation** (weeks 1–2) — measured on devnet (Task 8, M-B), a second position by the same wallet opened immediately after. Crank-asserted — risk #20, `mark_committed` does **not** check the existence of `Commitment` on L1 by itself |
| `write_disclosure` | L1, `#[action]` | creates `Disclosure [b"disclosure", hash]` **(seed — Task 8b, ruling 9)**, **checks `commitment_hash(args, salt) == Commitment.hash`**, otherwise `BadDisclosureHash`. The same `source_program` check. Called from `commit_aggregate` (above), not by a separate `reveal` instruction |

Delay — `Config.disclosure_delay_slots` (exists): minutes for the demo, 30 days for the product. Latency of commitment and reveal ≤ 5 min (the `commit_aggregate` step, measured M-B: `Commitment` is visible on base 1.1–1.4 s after the ER signature, `Disclosure` — after 3.0 s).

**2.4.2 `BalancesRoot` — a public debt receipt (approach A from the brainstorm)**

- Account `[b"balances_root"]`, **public**, delegated at bootstrap, committed together with `Pool`. **`#[account(zero_copy)] #[repr(C)]`** (Task 5, ruling 5 — not Borsh, details in §2.1/§4.1): `root_slot: u64, leaves: [[u8;32]; 64], version: u8, filled: u8, bump: u8, _pad: [u8;5]`.
- `set_balances_root(begin: bool, finalize: bool, padding_seed: [u8;32])` (ER, crank-gated, signature — Task 5/7/8): the crank passes `UserAccount`s as `remaining_accounts` (≤`ROOT_BATCH=16` per call, several calls for 64 slots — `begin` resets `root_slot`/`filled`, `finalize` fills the tail with padding). **The program itself** computes `leaf = keccak256(owner ‖ free_margin ‖ exit_salt ‖ root_slot)` from the account's real bytes → the crank **can skip** a user (blocks exit, risk #19), **cannot forge** a balance. Empty slots = `keccak256(padding_seed ‖ i)`; the seed is not stored on L1 → padding is indistinguishable from leaves, the exact N is hidden.
- `UserAccount.exit_salt: [u8;32]` — a new field; the client passes 32 random bytes into `init_user`.
- **Binding to `root_slot`** — all 64 leaves change on every commit regardless of activity → from outside it is not visible whose balance changed. **Measured (Task 8, M-E):** two consecutive cycles on real devnet-tee — all 64/64 leaves really changed between them. **Fixed 64 slots** → the number of users is not visible.
- **What it gives and what it does not.** The user locally (`keccak256` on the device) proves: "at slot S the protocol attested that X belongs to me". **A claim on L1 does not exist:** custody is eSPL, the Global Vault `2BX6ZNoX…` belongs to the program `SPLxh1LV…`, not `dexxer_core`; our program physically cannot sign a transfer out of it. Exit with a live TEE — `withdraw` + `undelegate_user`; exit without the TEE — a dependency on eSPL/MagicBlock, for which the root is the proof of debt. This is consistent with the trust model of §0.4 ("not from the operator") and with "there is no backend" (eSPL = the L1↔ER bridge).
- **Privacy:** the root publishes only a hash of `free_margin` — strictly more private than the raw `UserAccount` commit allowed by the week-1 spec. Not a single position field. The crank is already a member of all `UserAccount`s (liquidations) — the circle of those who see balances does not widen.

**2.4.3 `undelegate_user` — exit with a live TEE**

Owner, ER: `require!(Position.Empty && DisclosureQueue empty && free_margin == 0)` (first `withdraw`) → **scrub** of private fields (`UserAccount`: `session_key`/`session_expiry`/`actions_left`/`nonce`/`exit_salt`/`last_withdraw_slot`; `DisclosureQueue`: `head`/`len`/`records`) → **explicit `exit()`** on all three scrubbed accounts (ruling 10 below — critical, otherwise Anchor's automatic post-handler `exit()` rewrites the account already after the owner change and the runtime throws `ExternalAccountDataModified`) → `CloseEphemeralPermissionCpi` ×3 (the accounts become public — safe, because they are empty) → `MagicIntentBundleBuilder.commit_and_undelegate` of the three PDAs → on L1 the accounts return to the program.

Fix: three explicit `a.user_account.exit(&crate::ID)?; a.position.exit(&crate::ID)?; a.dq.exit(&crate::ID)?;` right after the scrub, before the CPI. **2.6.1 P3 — liquidations without the relayer: a per-position scheduler task (closes #18 completely)**

- Week 4 measured: the global `schedule_crank` ticks without `remaining_accounts`, therefore it does not liquidate. Decision — **one scheduler task per position with a fixed set of accounts**, registered by the program itself.
- A new ER instruction `liquidation_check`: signer — `Config.scheduler_signer` (`crank_signer_pda(admin)`, as in `crank_tick`); accounts `[config, market, market_risk, pool_live, oracle_feed, position, user_account]` — all delegated or read-only (not a single writable non-delegated one — the scheduler limitation from week 2). Body = the liquidation branch of `crank_tick` for one pair; stale/invalid oracle → no-op; empty position → no-op.
- Registration — a `ScheduleCrankCpi` CPI inside `open_position`: `task_id = i64::from_le_bytes(keccak(position_pubkey)[0..8])`, `interval_ms = 5000`, `iterations = i64::MAX`; payer — the delegated `FeeEscrow` (as for commits). Re-registration with the same `task_id` = update (idempotent).
- Cancellation — `CancelCrankCpi` with the same `task_id` in `close_position`, `finalize_close` (liquidation) and `undelegate_user`.
- **Spike — the first task of the week** (`spikes/05-crank-tee`): (1) three parallel tasks with different `task_id`s tick; (2) the cost of registration/tick from `FeeEscrow`; (3) `cancel` via CPI from under the program goes through; (4) whether the task registry is visible outside the TEE (public RPC/L1). If (4) = visible, registration/cancellation at open/close becomes an event-driven leak → fallback: register `liquidation_check` once at `init_user` (the position slot) and never cancel (a tick on an empty position is a no-op); more expensive, but without events. The decision is recorded in `weeks0-5-history.md#week-5` before the redeploy.

**Implemented.** All four preconditions were measured positively (Task 0 spike) — **open-time** registration/cancel was chosen, not the fallback. The tick signer turned out to be **`crank_signer_pda(fee_escrow)`**, not `Config.scheduler_signer` (the task's authority is `ScheduleCrankCpi.payer`, not the external tx signer; the same `FeeEscrow` PDA that pays for registration). `task_context` needs no derivation — an inert writable placeholder, convention = the `Position` PDA. **M-G′ PASS on devnet:** liquidation without the relayer in 6.97 s — the scheduler in the TEE really liquidates, closing risk #18 completely (week 4 closed only the mark-backstop half).

**2.6.2 P2 — Close frees the position immediately; commitment and reveal come from the queue (reveal in one cycle at zero delay)**

- Currently the record of a closed trade lives in `Position.closed` until `mark_committed` (the next 5-min cycle), so a repeat Open waits a cycle, and reveal — two cycles. Change: `close_position`/`finalize_close` put the `ClosedRecord` (with `salt`) **immediately into the `DisclosureQueue`** with the record state `pending_commitment`, and `Position → Empty` in the same tx (`Position.closed` is no longer used as storage). The trader can open a new position immediately (M-J).
- `write_commitment` (Magic Action) reads the record from the `DisclosureQueue`, not from `Position`; `mark_committed` sets `commitment_written = true` on the **record**, without touching `Position`. With `Config.disclosure_delay_slots == 0` the crank adds for one record **two actions into one bundle** — `write_commitment` and `write_disclosure` (the same `DisclosureArgs`, the same `commitment_hash`, the same escrow-signer check); `MAX_ACTIONS_PER_COMMIT` 4 → 8 (bridge cap 28/29, week 3). With `delay > 0` — `write_disclosure` only after the due slot, as now.
- Capacity: `DQ_CAPACITY = 8` undisclosed trades per trader; with a full queue `close_position` returns `DisclosureQueueFull` (Close waits for the nearest reveal; the UI shows "Waiting for disclosure slots"). An event-driven commit is forbidden (it reveals the moment of closing).
- Relayer: `COMMIT_INTERVAL_TICKS` env (default 300; demo 60 ≈ 80 s; ≈0.2 SOL/hour on devnet). Expectations: Close → Open immediately (M-J); Close → "Revealed" ≤ one cycle (M-H).

**Implemented.** `MAX_ACTIONS_PER_COMMIT` raised to **8** as the program ceiling (not 4) — a queue of 8 records in the worst case needs 8 commits + 8 disclosures. **The real bridge ceiling is lower and content-dependent**, not a simple "count > N": measured (Task 7) 8 FAIL / 4 PASS on a real loaded backlog; the relayer's `COMMIT_MAX_ACTIONS` env is set to **4** (an evidence-backed default, not a strictly derived bound) with halve-and-retry and quarantine of queues that fail at every budget. **M-J PASS:** reopen right after close (1.7 s), the 9th close with a full ring — `QueueFull`, `Position` stays `Open`. **M-H PASS in substance:** with `disclosure_delay_slots=0` commit+disclosure landed in one bundle (both PDAs on L1 confirmed post-hoc), the exact landing time was not caught by the script (>103.8 s, narrow polling window, not a regression).

**2.6.3 P4 — Exit with disclosure debt (partial `undelegate_user`)**

- `undelegate_user`: `UserAccount` and `Position` exit when `Position.state == Empty` (after P2 the `Closed` state exists only inside the closing tx, so the condition = "no open position"); a `DisclosureQueue` with `len == 0` exits as now; with `len > 0` — it **stays delegated**, members → `[crank]` (owner/session are removed), `UserAccount.exited = true` (a new flag) is written before exit. The scrub of private `UserAccount`/`Position` fields — as in week 3.
- Crank: after the `write_disclosure` of the last record of a queue with an `exited` owner it calls a new crank-only ER instruction `close_orphan_queue` → `commit_and_undelegate` with zeroed fields, on L1 `close`, rent → `fee_payer` (it funded `extra` at `init_user`).
- Repeat onboarding of the same wallet with a live orphan queue: a separate owner instruction `init_user_reuse_queue` (the same context as `init_user`, but `disclosure_queue` is an existing delegated account without `init`; `init_if_needed` is forbidden); the client chooses it if the queue PDA already exists; it resets `exited` and does not touch the queue's contents.

### 2.7 Week 6 design, part 1: relayer SIWS sessions — `/sponsor` and `/nonce` only with an owner session (#27, partially) **(week 6, 27.09.2026)**

Source of decisions — the 27.09 brainstorm (week 6 backlog, B.1). Goal — **a relayer authentication foundation** before new backend functionality, **not** a full closure of #27: SIWS proves key ownership, and fresh keys are free, so a Sybil drain of the daily budget with fresh owner keys remains open (it needs a scarce resource — invite codes / Seeker Genesis Token / per-IP limit — as a separate decision). The `dexxer_core` program does not change; changes — `services/relayer` + minimal wiring in `app`.

**State before the change (read from the code).** `/sponsor` already verifies the owner signature (`tx.verifySignatures(false)`), so proof of key ownership is there. `/nonce` accepts `body.owner` **without any signature** and spends real SOL (≈0.003 SOL of rent for two nonce accounts) from the same daily budget — an anonymous POST of random pubkeys drains `SPONSOR_DAILY_SOL` even without generating keys. The app already does SIWS on Connect (`signIn({ uri, domain })`, `components/auth/auth-provider.tsx`), but the `SignInOutput` is discarded — the relayer never sees it.

**2.7.1 Protocol (relayer)**

- `POST /auth/challenge` → `{ nonce, issuedAt, expirationTime, domain, uri, statement, version }`; `nonce` — 16 random bytes in hex (alphanumeric, as SIWS requires), TTL 5 min, stored in the DB. More than 10 000 open (unconsumed and unexpired) challenges → 503 — a safeguard against table bloat via an unauthenticated endpoint.
- `POST /auth/siws` `{ address, signedMessage, signature }` (base64) → `{ token, owner, expiresAt }`. Checks in order: (1) `parseSignInMessage` (`@solana/wallet-standard-util`, the reference SIWS implementation) — not SIWS → 400; (2) `domain == SIWS_DOMAIN` and host of `uri == SIWS_DOMAIN`; (3) the message `address` == `body.address`, a valid pubkey; (4) `issuedAt` within ±5 min of `now`, `expirationTime` (if present) — in the future, `notBefore` (if present) — in the past; (5) ed25519 (`tweetnacl`), signature exactly 64 bytes; (6) **only after a valid signature** — atomic nonce consumption (`UPDATE … SET used_at = now() WHERE nonce = $1 AND used_at IS NULL AND expires_at > now() RETURNING`), unknown/used/expired → 401 (consumption after the signature — so that a garbage request cannot burn someone else's nonce); (7) session: 32 random bytes, in the DB — only `sha256(token)`, TTL `AUTH_SESSION_TTL_HOURS` (default 168 = 7 days).
- `chainId` is not checked: the relayer serves only devnet, and some wallets substitute their own — an extra rejection protects nothing.
- `SIWS_DOMAIN` — the MWA identity domain (`IDENTITY_DOMAIN` in the app; currently defaults to the relayer host, because `identity.uri` = relayer origin since week 5).
- Privacy: SIWS is signed for the identity domain — not usable as TEE auth (different challenge format); the session token opens only the relayer API and is not forwarded anywhere. This is **not** an owner/TEE token in the sense of the CLAUDE.md rule "no service other than the crank holds owner/session tokens".

**2.7.2 Storage and gate (relayer)**

- Migration `006_auth.sql`: `auth_challenges(nonce PK, expires_at, used_at NULL)`, `auth_sessions(token_hash PK, owner, created_at, expires_at)`; expired rows are deleted along the way on `createChallenge`/`createSession` (`expires_at < now() − 1 h`), without background jobs.
- `AuthStore` — an interface following the `SponsorStore` pattern (`createChallenge`, `countOpenChallenges`, `consumeChallenge`, `createSession`, `getSession`) + `pgAuthStore(pool)` + an in-memory implementation in tests.
- Middleware `requireSession(store)`: `Authorization: Bearer <token>` → `sha256` → session → `res.locals.sessionOwner`; otherwise 401.
- `/sponsor`: session → `checkWhitelist` → **session owner == tx signer** (otherwise 403) → `reserve` → budget. A request without a session does not reach `reserve` and does not burn the owner's slots.
- `/nonce`: the owner — from the session; `body.owner` is optional, if passed and ≠ the session owner → 403.
- Mounting — in the same `sponsorEnabled && pool` block as `/sponsor`/`/nonce` (Postgres required). Without `SIWS_DOMAIN` the relayer logs an error loudly and **does not mount** `/auth`, `/sponsor`, `/nonce` (fail-closed, as with `dusdcMint`) — a security gate cannot silently end up open.
- Env: `SIWS_DOMAIN` (required), `AUTH_SESSION_TTL_HOURS` (168). New relayer dependency — `@solana/wallet-standard-util` 1.1.4 (the same version already in the app tree). Logs: the rejection reason and the owner pubkey, **never** the token.

**2.7.3 App (minimal wiring)**

- `app/src/lib/relayerAuth.ts`: token in SecureStore per-owner (`{ token, expiresAt }`, `session.ts` pattern); `fetchChallenge()`, `exchangeSiws()`, `ensureRelayerSession(owner, signMessage)` — live token (> 5 min to `expiresAt`) → no-op, otherwise challenge → `createSignInMessageText` → `signMessage` (the same function as TEE auth in `er.ts`) → exchange; the signature is normalized by `pickSignature` (wallets return either bare 64 bytes or `message ‖ signature`). `domain`/`uri` in the signed message — **the app's own MWA identity** (`IDENTITY_DOMAIN`/`IDENTITY_URI`: these are exactly what the wallet checks, a mismatch would produce a foreign-domain warning); from the challenge only `nonce`/`issuedAt`/`expirationTime`/`statement`/`version` are taken. A mismatch between the relayer's `SIWS_DOMAIN` and the app identity is a configuration error: the relayer will reject the exchange (401 domain mismatch), the app warns in the log under `__DEV__`.
- Connect (`auth-provider.tsx`): challenge before `signIn`, in the payload — `nonce`/`issuedAt`/`expirationTime`/`statement`; after — `exchangeSiws`. The same single prompt. A relayer error at this step does not fail Connect — the session will be fetched later by `ensureRelayerSession`.
- `runBatchedOnboarding` and `depositTx` — `ensureRelayerSession` at the very start, **before the first prompt**: `signOwnerL1` on any `/nonce` error silently falls back to a live blockhash, and for Phantom that is "confirm timeout" (Alpenglow ≈25 s vs ≈38 s of prompt) — a late 401 would break Phantom unnoticed.
- `sponsorTx`/`fetchNonces` add `Authorization: Bearer`; on 401 — erase the stored token and throw a clear error (no auto-prompt mid-flow; the next attempt of the flow will take a new session).
- **Relayer unavailable → degradation, not a block** (final self-review, 27.09): if a session cannot be issued on the relayer side — network (0), no `/auth` (404 — a relayer before §2.7), 5xx — the flow continues without a session, exactly as before sessions with the relayer down (`/nonce` → live blockhash, sponsored legs fail with a relayer error); a self-funded owner is not blocked. 4xx from `/auth/siws` and a wallet rejection — a flow error. As a side effect: a new APK against an old relayer works as before.
- `@solana/wallet-standard-util` 1.1.4 — an explicit dependency of `app/package.json` (currently only transitive).
- Prompts: a new user — unchanged; already connected after the update — +1 on the first onboarding/deposit, then once every 7 days.
- Deliberately not: `/auth/logout`, deleting the token on Disconnect (tokens are per-owner, they cannot conflict; the server session expires by TTL).

### 2.8 Week 6 design, part 2: multi-market — BTC/ETH/HYPE/ZEC alongside SOL **(week 6, 28.09.2026)**

Source of decisions — the 28.09 brainstorm (week 6 backlog, C.1). Goal — a trader with one dUSDC margin holds positions on several USD perps at once; the pool remains the single counterparty, the privacy model is unchanged. User decisions: **(1)** USD perps only (cross pairs like SOL/BTC — a separate design later); **(2)** markets are added by an admin instruction without redeploying the relayer/app; **(3)** margin is **isolated** (as now — `Position.margin` per position); **(4)** a `Position` PDA per market (seeds `[position, owner, market]` already exist), not a single account with slots; **(5)** positions on **all** markets are created together and **not at the moment of a trade** (see "Privacy").

**State before the change (measured 28.09).** The MagicBlock oracle on devnet (`PriCems5…`, ER; base — a delegated clone) publishes live Pyth Lazer USD feeds: **BTC (1), ETH (2), SOL (6), ZEC (66), HYPE (110)** (+ USDC 7, USDT 8, BONK 9, JUP 92) — updated in a batch ≈ once every 9 s. **WBTC (103)** — the account exists, the price is zero (unusable); **WETH (631)**, cross feeds **ETH/BTC (419)**, **SOL/ETH (564)** — not published; there is no direct SOL/BTC even in Pyth Lazer. Hence "usdc/weth" → ETH-PERP, "usdc/wbtc" → BTC-PERP. In the program a single market is hard-wired only in `init_market`/`delegate_market` (`SOL_SYMBOL` in the seeds) and in onboarding (`init_user` creates a `Position` only for SOL); `Trade`/`crank_tick`/`liquidation_check`/`schedule_crank` are already parameterized by the market account; `undelegate_user` is gated by `free_margin == 0 && locked_margin == 0`, i.e. it is already impossible while a position is open on **any** market.

**Privacy (decision (5)).** The `Position` PDA is derived from `[position, owner, market]` and created on L1, where there is no filter. If a position on a market were created at the first trade, L1 would show which markets the trader trades and when they started, and the relayer operator would see the market choice via `/sponsor` — a violation of "on L1 only the onboarding bytes are visible". Therefore each user gets positions on **all** markets: at onboarding, and for markets added later — at app launch, in a single batch. The set and the creation time are the same for everyone and do not correlate with trades. Price — rent per market per user from the `fee_payer` budget (to measure, §2.8.5).

### 2.9 Week 6 design, part 3: position slots, no trade disclosure, clean start **(week 6, 30.09.2026)**

Source of decisions — the 30.09 brainstorm after completing plan 2 (branch `multi-market-relayer`, not merged). This section **replaces** §2.4.1 (the 13F pipeline), §2.6 in the part about exit with disclosure debt, and §2.8.2 (positions per market); §2.8.1 (market = symbol), §2.8.3 (market registry, crank/indexer per market) and §2.8.5 remain in force with the amendments below. User decisions: **(1)** trader positions — fixed slots in a single account, the market in the data, not in the address; **(2)** 16 slots (initially 8; raised to 16 the same day, before implementation — after deploy a change in the number of slots would mean an account migration); **(3)** trades are **not disclosed** at all — the 18.09 architectural decision "disclosure — commit-then-reveal" is cancelled; **(4)** the trader's own history — a private ring of 16 records in the ER; **(5)** a clean devnet start (new program address), without migration; **(6)** in the same deploy — risks #38 and #39.

**Why not a PDA per market.** The seed `[position, owner, market]` puts the market into the L1 account address. Consequences: a new market = a new L1 account for every trader; creation at the first trade discloses the market choice, so positions have to be handed out to everyone in advance. Plan 2 did this with the relayer (variant C: `fee_payer` creates, the crank makes it private) — it works, but costs ≈0.011 SOL of frozen protocol rent per owner, worsens Sybil #27 and requires a background loop, a balance threshold, a janitor and a cooldown. Slots remove the cause: a new market — one admin tx, zero actions per trader; on L1 everyone has one identical account. The same pattern in Drift (`perpPositions` — a fixed array of 8). MagicBlock ephemeral accounts were rejected: they cannot be the source of truth for balances (they disappear with the ER state).

**Why no disclosure.** Disclosure is a product decision, not a system requirement: `Disclosure.owner` is always zero, i.e. it was protocol transparency, not trader accountability. The cost — the most expensive subsystem after the core: a per-trader queue that limits the number of closed trades per disclosure window (8 per 30 days), risk #37, the inability to liquidate with a full ring, the bridge limit of 4 actions per commit, queue quarantine. What stays public is the coarsened aggregate `Pool` and `BalancesRoot` — they still prove the protocol's liabilities and their backing. Lost: external verifiability of individual trades and the "13F" thesis in positioning.

**2.9.1 Trader accounts**

Two accounts instead of three, both created at onboarding, delegated, permission `[owner, session, crank]`.

- `UserAccount` (Borsh) — as before, plus `rent_payer: Pubkey` (#39) and `_reserved: [u8; 32]`; without `nonce`.
- `Positions` — `#[account(zero_copy)] #[repr(C)]`, seeds `[b"positions", owner]`, access via `AccountLoader` (3.1 KiB Borsh by value breaks the SBF stack — the `BalancesRoot` lesson, week 3). Layout: `owner: Pubkey | slots: [PositionSlot; 16] | history: [HistoryRecord; 16] | history_head: u8 | history_len: u8 | version: u8 | bump: u8 | _pad: [u8; 4] | _reserved: [u8; 64]` — 3176 B + 8 discriminator; rent ≈0.023 SOL, once, in the existing onboarding leg.
  - `PositionSlot` (96 B): `market: Pubkey | size | entry | margin | liq_price | opened_slot | oi_notional | last_liq_sample: u64 ×7 | state: u8 (0 Empty, 1 Open) | side: u8 | liq_ticks: u8 | _pad: [u8; 5]`.
  - `HistoryRecord` (96 B): `market: Pubkey | size | entry | exit: u64 | pnl: i64 | fees | opened_slot | closed_slot: u64 | side: u8 | reason: u8 (0 User — full close by the owner, 1 Liquidated, 2 — partial `decrease_position`; constants `HISTORY_REASON_*`, 0/1 equal `CloseReason::as_u8()`) | _pad: [u8; 6]`.
- One position per market per trader; the slot is looked up by the market key; `open` takes the first `Empty`, all occupied → `NoFreeSlot`. History overwrites the oldest record (nothing blocks) and **is erased before exiting the ER** — not a single private byte reaches L1.
- Gone: `Position`, `DisclosureQueue`, `ClosedRecord`, `Commitment`, `Disclosure`. `Config` loses `disclosure_delay_slots`. `MarketRisk`/`Pool`/`PoolLive`/`BalancesRoot`/`FeeEscrow` — unchanged; `Market` gets two fields for #38 — `last_print: u64` (`posted_slot` of the last print that `crank_tick` **accepted**, i.e. on which the deviation safeguard did not fire) and `sample_seq: u64` (a counter of distinct accepted prints), only `crank_tick` moves them. Error codes are only appended (`NoFreeSlot` 6049, `PositionLiquidatable` 6050 — final review), unused ones stay in the enum marked "retired/unused" — numbers are stable for the app's error map.

**2.9.2 Instructions**

- **Trading:** `open/increase/decrease/close_position`, `add_margin` — the market account + slot lookup. `Trade`: `[signer, config, market, market_risk, pool_live, user_account, positions, feed, fee_escrow, task_context(=positions), magic_program, liq_crank_signer]` — 12 accounts. `finalize_close` writes a `HistoryRecord` and resets the slot; there is no path by which a close could fail due to fullness. The task of a **liquidated** position stays alive (a tick over an empty slot is a no-op), as today: it is refreshed by the next `open_position` on the same market or cancelled by `undelegate_user`: the latter accepts **no more than 16** markets in `remaining_accounts` (taken only by key), so the client passes the markets where the trader may still have a task — markets from the history ring plus the known current ones, not "all markets of the registry"; the program cancels the `task_id` of each (cancel of an unknown id — a measured safe no-op, week 5). A task left over an exited account is functionally a no-op (there is no slot), its cost is not measured — plan 4. **#38 (final mechanism):** a liquidation tick is a new **price sample**, not a call and not a slot: `liq_ticks` grows only when `Market.sample_seq > slot.last_liq_sample`; `sample_seq` is incremented in `crank_tick` only on a print that the tick **accepted** (the deviation safeguard did not fire — C3), and only when the feed's `posted_slot` **differs** from `Market.last_print` (identity, not order — C2: with `>` a single high `posted_slot` would freeze all subsequent samples and liquidations; staleness is gated separately by `publish_time`); `open_position` writes the current `sample_seq` into the slot (only prints after the open count). The `Market.mark_slot` key was rejected: it grows on every crank tick, so two crank sources (the relayer and the scheduled `crank_tick` without candidates) in adjacent ER slots would give two ticks per print. Default `liq_hysteresis_ticks` 3 → 2. Consequence: `liquidation_check` only reads the market and does not move the sample, so liquidation without the relayer **requires a live `crank_tick` source** (the market's scheduled `i64::MAX` crank); without any crank nothing gets liquidated (a frozen mark is not grounds).
- **Commit:** `commit_aggregate()` — `commit(&[Pool, BalancesRoot])` via `FeeEscrow`, without actions, without `max_actions`, without `remaining_accounts`.
- **Exit:** `undelegate_user` — gate `open_count() == 0` and `free_margin == 0 && locked_margin == 0`, zeroes all slots (defence in depth — they are zero anyway) and the history, `exited = true`, explicit `exit()` only for `user_account` before `commit_and_undelegate` of both accounts (ruling 10): `Positions` is zero-copy, its automatic `exit` rewrites only the same 8-byte discriminator (a conclusion from the Anchor code, not measured on devnet). `rent_payer` and `_reserved` in `UserAccount` are kept deliberately (`rent_payer` is public on L1 anyway). `delegate_user` on an exited account — `UserExited` (was `NotExited`). There is no partial exit. `close_exited_user` (L1) — signer `Config.fee_payer` **or the owner**, rent → `UserAccount.rent_payer` (**#39**). Re-onboarding — a regular `init_user` after the close.

**2.10.1 Timeframes and bucketing**

16 timeframes: `1s 1m 5m 15m 30m 1h 2h 4h 6h 8h 12h 24h 2D 5D 1W 1M` (labels — as in the backlog; `24h`, not `1D`). The pure logic exists in two TS copies — `services/relayer/src/indexer/timeframes.ts` and `app/src/features/chart/timeframes.ts` — and a shared set of golden vectors pins them (the same pattern as the `hashes` of week 3).

- `bucketStart(tf, ms)`: for all except `1W`/`1M` — `floor(ms / width) * width` from the epoch (`2D`/`5D` also from the epoch, not from Monday — pinned by a vector); `1W` — Monday 00:00 UTC (the epoch is a Thursday, so not a multiple); `1M` — the first day of the month 00:00 UTC.
- `prevBucket(tf, t)` — start of the previous bucket; for `1M` — month arithmetic (`Date.UTC(y, m − 1, 1)`), for the rest — `t − width`. `nthPrevBucket(tf, t, n)` — iteration.
- `tierOf(tf)`: `1s → ticks`; `1m 5m 15m 30m → 1m`; `1h 2h 4h 6h 8h 12h → 1h`; `24h 2D 5D 1W 1M → 1d`. Each tf is a whole number of tier buckets (calendar ones — variable).
- `mergeCandles(lower: Candle[], tf)`: groups by `bucketStart(tf, c.t)`; `o` of the first, `h` max, `l` min, `c` of the last; the input must be sorted by `t`.
- Golden vectors: year boundary (31.12 → 01.01), leap February 2028, Sunday 23:59:59.999 UTC → Monday, `2D` on an odd day from the epoch, `1M` from 31 to 30 days, `1s` on a millisecond boundary.

**2.10.2 Schema and writes**

- Migration `009_candles.sql`: `candles(market text, tf text, t bigint, o bigint, h bigint, l bigint, c bigint, source text NOT NULL, PRIMARY KEY (market, tf, t))`, `tf ∈ {'1m','1h','1d'}` (CHECK). `source ∈ {'oracle','pyth_pro'}`.
- The same migration once rolls up the existing ticks (≈655 thousand, SOL since 22.09) into three tiers in SQL: `(array_agg(price ORDER BY ts))[1]` → `o`, `[array_length]` → `c`, `MIN`/`MAX` → `l`/`h`, grouping by `(ts / width) * width`; `source = 'oracle'`, `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` (re-running the migration is safe). It runs before retention, so the history of our own ticks is not lost.
- Writes: `insertTick` is extended to a single query that inserts the tick and does three upserts into `candles` (`ON CONFLICT (market, tf, t) DO UPDATE SET h = GREATEST(candles.h, EXCLUDED.h), l = LEAST(candles.l, EXCLUDED.l), c = EXCLUDED.c, source = 'oracle'`; `o` does not change). The oracle candle beats the backfill also by `source` (the upsert sets `'oracle'` if the bucket was already from Pyth Pro — from then on it is live).
- Retention: in the relayer's commit cycle (`createCycleRunner`, a step under `runIsolated`, every `COMMIT_INTERVAL_MS`) — `DELETE FROM ticks WHERE ts < $now − TICKS_RETENTION_MS`; env `TICKS_RETENTION_MS` (default 604800000 = 7 days, min. 3600000). `candles` without retention (`1m` ≈ 525 thousand rows/market/year — acceptable). The first pass after deploy will delete ≈600 thousand rows with one `DELETE` — expected seconds, Railway Postgres will cope; if not — chunk by `ts` (decision at deploy).

## 3. Margin math and liquidation

### 3.1 Units

| Quantity | Type | Precision |
|---|---|---|
| Price | `u64` | 1e6 |
| Size | `u64` | 1e9 |
| Collateral / notional / PnL | `u64` / `i64` | 1e6 |
| Rates | `u16`/`u32` | bps |

Intermediate `u128`, `checked_*`, conversion in `math.rs`. Rounding in favour of the pool: fees and requirements up, payouts down.

### 3.2 Formulas

```
notional      = size × mark / 1e9
upnl (long)   = size × (mark − entry) / 1e9
upnl (short)  = size × (entry − mark) / 1e9
equity        = margin + upnl − close_fee(notional)
liq_price
  long:  entry × (1 − 1/lev_eff + mmr)     lev_eff = notional / margin
  short: entry × (1 + 1/lev_eff − mmr)
```

Entry: `margin ≥ IMR × notional`, `lev ≤ max_lev`, `size ≥ min_size`, `OI_side + size ≤ oi_cap`, `notional ≤ max_position`. `increase` — entry as VWAP. `decrease` — remainder ≥ IMR or full close, PnL proportionally.

### 3.3 SOL-PERP parameters (in the `Market` account)

| Parameter | Start |
|---|---|
| max_lev | 10x |
| IMR | 1000 bps |
| MMR | 500 bps |
| open/close fee | 6 bps |
| liquidation fee | 100 bps notional |
| oi_cap | 30% of pool capital per side |
| max_staleness | 2 s — count by ER `Clock` / `publish_time`, **not** by `posted_slot` (check 4, 19.09.2026) |
| max_conf | 50 bps; `conf == 0` = "not filled" → hard reject on open (check 4). **(week 1, 20.09.2026)** `Market.max_conf_bps == 0` disables the conf check completely (`oracle::check_open_quality`, the guard is skipped before `require!(conf > 0)`) — new risk #11 in §7.1: the demo config sets `max_conf_bps = 0`, because the devnet feed consistently returns `conf == 0` |
| ema_alpha | for a 1–2 s period; the actual crank interval floats, see §3.5 |
| hysteresis | 2 **ticks** (not seconds — the tick interval is not fixed, check 5, 19.09.2026) |

**SOL/USD oracle — measured (check 4, 19.09.2026).** Feed `ENYwebBThHzmzwPLAQvCucUTsjyfBSZdD9ViXksS4jPu` = PDA from seeds `["price_feed", "pyth-lazer", "6"]` under `PriCems5tHihc6UDXDjzjeawomAwBduWMGAi8ZUjppd`.

- **Feed identity — only through PDA derivation** from `Config.oracle_program` + seeds. `writeAuthority` in the account data is the System Program (`1111…1111`), i.e. never set; it is unusable as proof of origin.
- **`exponent` is stored as `+8`**, not `−8`: price = `price / 10^exponent` (raw `11282999668`, expo `8` → ≈ $112.83). Never `price × 10^exponent`.
- **`conf` was `0` on every read** (base and TEE). We treat zero as "not filled", not as "zero uncertainty" → hard reject on `open`/`increase`; on close and liquidation — does not block.
- **`posted_slot` is an ER slot** (~317 million vs ~500 million on L1). It cannot be compared with an L1 slot; staleness is counted by ER `Clock` and `publish_time`.
- Account size — **134 bytes**: 133 per IDL `PriceUpdateV2` (`verificationLevel` = `Full`) + 1 trailing byte `0x00` of unknown purpose. It does not affect decoding of the fields up to and including `posted_slot`, but the parser must read by offsets, not by length.
- On base devnet the same account exists but is dead (`price = 0`, `posted_slot = 0`, owner — Pyth receiver). It is fresh **only inside the TEE** (owner — `PriCems…`, `age = 0 s`).

### 3.4 Mark vs index

Index — Pyth Lazer via the Pricing Oracle. Mark — EMA(index) in `Market.mark`, `mark_slot`. Liquidation by mark; entry/exit by index at tx time + slippage. Deviation guard `|index − mark| > X%` → `paused_open`, closes allowed. Trade-off: EMA lags in a trend → liquidation later than by index.

**Implemented (week 1, 20.09.2026).** Two separate places, both in `u128`/`checked_*`:

- `oracle::check_deviation` — on `open_position`/`increase_position`, right after `check_open_quality`: if `Market.mark > 0` and `|index − mark| · 10 000 / mark > Market.max_deviation_bps`, → `DexxerError::OracleDeviation`. `close`/`decrease` never call this check — exit must always remain available.
- `crank_tick` — snapshot of `prev_mark` before the EMA update, `dev_bps` is computed against `prev_mark` (not against the just-updated mark, otherwise this tick's EMA masks its own deviation). When triggered: `paused_open = true`, mark is still updated by EMA (so that the guard resolves itself when mark catches up with index), but the candidate loop for this tick is skipped (`return Ok(())`) — there are no liquidations on a tick whose index deviated. First tick (`mark == 0`) — seed `mark = index`, no check.

### 3.5 Crank

Cycle ~1 s: (1) oracle + validation; stale → skip liquidations, mark not updated; (2) mark-EMA; (3) candidates from buckets `±δ` from mark in `MarketRisk`; (4) `equity < MMR × notional` for two ticks → `liquidate`: close at mark, liq fee into the pool, remainder → `free_margin`, `ClosedRecord{Liquidated}`; (5) bad debt → `Pool.insurance`, then capital, `bad_debt_total` public via the commit; (6) the `crank_tick` signer — `Config.crank` or the `CRANK_SIGNER` PDA (`magicblock_magic_program_api::pda`); verification of the scheduler executor — week 2 on devnet **(week 1, 20.09.2026)**.

**Candidates (week 1, 20.09.2026).** `MarketRisk.buckets` is not implemented (§8 Q4, §2.1) — liquidation candidates are passed explicitly in `remaining_accounts` as pairs `[Position, UserAccount]`, ≤16 pairs (`MAX_CANDIDATES`), each account is checked for program ownership, the writable flag and PDA derivation match before deserialization (`programs/dexxer_core/src/instructions/crank.rs`). Choosing candidates to pass is the responsibility of the caller (crank/fallback script), not of buckets in `MarketRisk`.

Trigger — MagicBlock scheduler; fallback — `scripts/crank-fallback` with the `Config.crank` key.

**(week 2, 21.09.2026) Candidates without a registry: crank as a permission member.** M2 (`weeks0-5-history.md#week-2` Task 1) confirmed by measurement: `getProgramAccounts` with the crank token returns private `Position` accounts of which the crank is a member (memcmp on the account discriminator). The `MarketRisk.traders` registry (the §8 Q4 question about scale) **is not added** — `scripts/crank-fallback/index.ts` finds candidates via gPA + crank token, as planned in §2.1/§4.2, not via a separate index. Client-side filter (not a program change): stale `UserAccount` accounts (smaller layout, left by earlier test runs) are skipped before building `remaining_accounts` — otherwise one undecodable candidate would abort the whole tick via the early `?`-return in `crank_tick`.

**(week 2, 21.09.2026) The scheduler really ticks, the signer is a per-authority PDA, not `Config.crank`/the flat `CRANK_SIGNER`.** Three consecutive findings on real devnet-tee (`weeks0-5-history.md#week-2` Task 6, three fix rounds): (1) the scheduled execution of `crank_tick` is signed by the Magic Program itself, with the value `crank_signer_pda(authority)` = `find_program_address(["crank-executor", authority], CRANK_PROGRAM_ID)`, where `authority` is the payer (`admin`) of the `schedule_crank` transaction, not the flat `CRANK_SIGNER` or the `Config.scheduler_signer` placeholder from Task 1 M1; (2) a writable, non-delegated account (here — `Config`) is forbidden in `ScheduleCrankCpi`'s `instruction_accounts` unconditionally — writing `crank_signer_pda(admin)` into `Config` **in the same transaction** that registers the crank is structurally impossible; (3) resolved by moving the write to a separate base admin instruction `set_scheduler_signer` (the same `AdminConfig` pattern as `pause`/`unpause`), called **before** `schedule_crank`. After that: `Market.mark_slot` grows monotonically (+18 300 slots in 183 s) without any external fallback process — the first direct confirmation that the Magic Actions ER scheduler executes `crank_tick` on `dexxer_core` on its own. `crank_tick`'s own three-way signer constraint (`Config.crank` / `Config.scheduler_signer` / flat `CRANK_SIGNER`) stayed unchanged all three rounds — it now works through the second branch, now that `Config.scheduler_signer` actually contains `crank_signer_pda(admin)`.

**Clarification of liq fee / insurance (week 1, 20.09.2026).** The liquidation trigger (step 4) computes equity with `close_fee_bps` (6 bps) — `risk::liquidatable_now` → `math::equity(margin, upnl, close_fee)`, threshold `eq/notional < mmr_bps`. But the actual settlement (step 4, `finalize_close`) charges `liq_fee_bps` (100 bps), not `close_fee_bps`. With default parameters (`mmr_bps=500`, `close_fee_bps=6`, `liq_fee_bps=100`) this gives the trader an effective "cushion" of ≈4.06 % notional at the trigger boundary (margin+upnl at the boundary = (mmr_bps+close_fee_bps)=506 bps notional; after deducting liq_fee_bps=100 bps there remains `to_user` ≈406 bps = 4.06 % notional) — liquidation right at the MMR boundary does not zero out the trader's margin. Step 5 is simplified for week 1: `insurance` only accumulates `liq_fee_bps` (`risk::settle_into_pool`, branch `liquidation`), **is not** spent on bad debt — the spec line below "bad debt → `Pool.insurance`, then capital" is deferred for week 1: the shortfall goes straight from `protocol_liquidity` (`settle_into_pool`, `checked_sub` without falling back to `insurance`), `bad_debt_total` — statistics only.

**Measured on devnet-tee (check 5, 19.09.2026):** with a request of `execution_interval_millis = 1000`, **27 ticks** occurred in 20 s (~740 ms average interval, ≈1.35 ticks/s). That is, the requested interval is a **floor, not a period**: ticks arrive earlier than ordered, and an even step is not guaranteed. Consequences, mandatory to follow:

- any hysteresis and any "after N seconds" — count **in ticks**, not in seconds; nothing downstream has the right to assume a fixed delta between ticks;
- the CU/quota budget is computed from the **upper** bound of the frequency, not from the requested one;
- the production `crank_tick` **must** validate that the caller is the expected crank signer PDA of the delegation program (the vendor example does not do this: its `increment` is permissionless, anyone can call the liquidation logic out of turn);
- the product must have a **cancel path** for the scheduled task — the spike had none, the task simply self-terminated after the 30th iteration.

### 3.6 Invariants (proptest + LiteSVM)

**(week 1, 20.09.2026)** The invariant is rewritten for `Pool.protocol_liquidity` (§2.1, §4.1):

```
protocol_liquidity + Σ free_margin + Σ position.margin + fees_accrued + insurance == capital_total == pool eATA balance
```

`bad_debt_total` — statistics, a separate counter; it moves no tokens and is not included in the invariant sum (checked by every LiteSVM trading/crank test (`trade`, `resize`, `crank`, `invariants`) via `assert_invariant`/`assert_invariant_ctx`, `tests/litesvm/src/lib.rs`; `admin`/`smoke`/`user` do not call this check — there are no trading actions there that affect the invariant).

- `liq_price(long) < entry < liq_price(short)` at equal lev.
- Rounding does not shrink the pool.
- Liquidation does not give the user more than `margin + upnl`.
- After `close`/`liquidate`: `Position` zeroed, `oi_side −= size`.

### 3.7 Known weaknesses

MMR 5% at 10x — a 5% move in 1–4 s = bad debt for the pool (test). EMA lags. A single oracle; a deviation guard against a second source — v1.

### 4.4 Modules


**The `math.rs` / `risk.rs` boundary.** `math.rs` — pure formulas without Anchor types and without policy: `notional`, `upnl`, `fee`, `required_margin`, `equity`, `vwap_entry`, `decrease_pnl`, **`liq_price`**, **`ema`** (mark-EMA §3.4). All rates are `u32` bps, intermediates `u128`, every step `checked_*`, rounding in the pool's favor. `risk.rs` — policy on top of them: IMR/MMR checks, `is_liquidatable` in the market context, `MarketRisk` buckets, selection of ≤16 candidates per tick. `math.rs` returns `MathError`; conversion to `anchor_lang::error::Error` — via `From<MathError>` → `DexxerError` in `errors.rs`, so that the formulas do not know about Anchor.

**`oracle.rs` — from check 4 (19.09.2026).** Verify the feed by **PDA derivation** `["price_feed", "pyth-lazer", <symbol>]` under `Config.oracle_program`, not by `writeAuthority` (it = System Program, empty). Price = `price / 10^exponent`, `exponent` on this feed is `+8`. `conf == 0` → rejection on open. Staleness — by the ER `Clock`/`publish_time`; `posted_slot` is an ER slot, not compared with an L1 slot. The account is 134 bytes (133 IDL + 1 trailing) — read by offsets, do not validate by length.

**(week 1, 20.09.2026)** `check_open_quality` implements a switch: `market.max_conf_bps == 0` returns `Ok(())` immediately, without checking `conf_bps > 0` and without comparing with the limit — this is a deliberate market parameter (`MarketParams`), not a bug, needed because the devnet feed consistently returns `conf == 0` (check 4). Risk #11 §7.1.

Anchor rules: `has_one`/`seeds`/`bump`/`constraint` on every account; do not use `init_if_needed`; the oracle `program_id` — from `Config`; validate `remaining_accounts` explicitly; `version: u8` in every PDA.

- **Every ER instruction checks the signer itself.** `EphemeralPermission` gates only reads; neither `getAuthToken` nor tx submission checks membership (check 9, 19.09.2026). Therefore authorization is always in the program logic: `has_one`/`constraint` against `Config.crank`, `Position.owner`, `UserAccount.session_key`, plus `session_expiry` and `actions_left`. "The account is permissioned" is never a reason to skip a check.
- **Every `#[action]` context declares `source_program`.** The account order must be `[...data, source_program, escrow_auth, escrow]`, and `source_program` is pinned with `#[account(address = crate::ID)]`. The delegation program inserts the id of the destination program as a separate account during CPI dispatch, while the `#[action]` macro (SDK 0.16.2) appends only `escrow_auth`/`escrow`. Without an explicit field all accounts after the data shift by one, and the action rejects itself as `Unauthorized` (check 7, 19.09.2026 — this is exactly how `update_leaderboard` failed in the spike).

A plain wallet with base SOL has a visible balance in the ER too (cloned together with the rest of the state) — exactly this is enough for the session to pay its own ER fees, as intended. Onboarding states (Task 7 `useOnboarding.ts`): `Disconnected → NotOnboarded → Funded → Initialized → Delegated → Credited → Permissioned → SessionSet`, each step idempotent (checks on-chain state before executing). Trading actions (Task 8): `openPosition`/`closePosition` are signed **only** by the session `Keypair` locally, without any MWA prompt — the `Trade`/`Position` screens (§5.5) read the session stored by onboarding, never generate a new one (a new session without `set_session` would give `Unauthorized`).

**Membership in a permission ≠ the right to act (check 9, 19.09.2026).** Three different checks that are easy to confuse: (1) issuing the auth token (`getAuthToken`) — only a signature, membership is not checked, anyone gets a token; (2) **reading** a permissioned account — gated by membership, a non-member sees `null`; (3) **tx submission and execution** — not gated by anything except the instruction's own logic. In check 9 the session key was not a member (reading gave `null`), but its tx successfully changed the counter. Consequence for us: the session key must be **both** in `members` (so the client can read `Position`/`UserAccount`) **and** checked in the program via `UserAccount.session_key` + `session_expiry` + `actions_left` — membership alone is not enough, it forbids nothing.

1. ~~The mechanism linking an L1 deposit to `credit_deposit` in ER~~ **CLOSED by week 1 (20.09.2026), details — `docs/superpowers/plans/weeks0-5-history.md#week-1` Task 13 Q1.** Answer: there is no separate "link" at all. `credit_deposit` is an owner-signed ER instruction that itself does the CPI SPL-transfer user eATA → pool eATA and, in the same transaction, `free_margin += amount`; no crank, callback or `l1_signature` is needed.
